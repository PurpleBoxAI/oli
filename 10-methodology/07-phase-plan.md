# 7. Phase plan

Six phases from "get ready" to "monitor and improve". Each phase ends at a gate. A missed gate pauses the next phase; it does not skip it. Durations are examples; a single location with an engaged owner has been estimated at about 12 weeks end to end, which is an estimate, not a commitment.

Each phase lists who does it: owner alone, or owner plus partner. In the do-it-yourself path, "partner" is the owner.

## Phase 0: Get ready (example: week 1, plus a two-week baseline)

Activities:
- Agree goals and what success looks like, in the owner's words.
- Complete the self-assessment (`09-self-assessment.md`).
- Choose the AI stack (see `20-implementation/`).
- Inventory accounts and access: Google Ads, Analytics, Business Profile, website, email, calendar, spreadsheet. Record who owns each one. For a franchise, ask the franchisor who holds the Google accounts and which outside vendors are allowed.
- Check eligibility for pay-per-lead local ads where they exist (some categories require a license check).
- Record the baseline (`06-metrics.md`).
- Agree the minimum-data rule and who can open the Lead Log.

Gate: goals written, access known, baseline recorded, go or no-go decided.

## Phase 1: Foundation (example: weeks 1 to 2)

Activities:
- Clean up the Business Profile: hours, services, service-area ZIPs, photos.
- Create the Lead Log from the template, in the owner's account.
- Fill the rules file with the business facts; owner approves.
- Approve the intake script (owner, and franchisor for brand wording).
- Set up call answering with the script; forward unanswered calls; connect it to the Lead Log. Place test calls, one per branch of the script.
- Connect the AI stack to email, calendar and the Lead Log, in the owner's accounts. Install the workflows.
- Run the verification checklist from the implementation guide.

Gate: a test inquiry flows from a call to a Lead Log row to a drafted follow-up, with no manual copying.

## Phase 2: Lead follow-up live (example: weeks 2 to 4)

Activities:
- Workflow A runs every business day.
- Owner reviews drafts, makes calls, updates Status and First callback.
- Weekly 15-minute check: what broke, what was slow, what the owner skipped.

Gate: two weeks of daily runs with time to first callback recorded for every lead.

## Phase 3: Visibility (example: weeks 3 to 6)

Activities:
- Workflow C runs weekly: review replies and one post.
- Workflow B runs weekly: ads review, read-only or from the exported report.
- If ads are not running yet, start one tight campaign on the service ZIPs, or pay-per-lead local ads if eligible.

Gate: four consecutive weekly ads reviews, with at least one recommended change applied and checked the following week.

## Phase 4: Reporting and tuning (example: weeks 6 to 8)

Activities:
- First monthly owner report (Workflow D) against the baseline.
- First intake script tuning round (Workflow E); owner applies approved edits.

Gate: a one-page report the owner agrees is true, with every gap named.

## Phase 5: Monitor, manage and improve (ongoing)

Activities:
- Keep the cadence: daily A, weekly B and C, monthly D and E.
- Monthly: compare against baseline, pick three fixes, log every change made.
- Quarterly: review the rules file, the service list, the ZIP list and the script; re-run the verification checklist; check that connections still work after any tool update.
- Keep a change log: date, what changed, why, result seen the following month.
- Exit or hand-over: if a partner ran this phase, hand the change log, the rules file and the checklist to the owner. Everything already lives in the owner's accounts, so nothing moves.

Gate: none. This phase runs as long as the business wants it.

## Risks to watch from Phase 0

- The franchisor controls the Google accounts or bans outside vendors: ask first; offer the package to the franchisor instead.
- Not eligible for pay-per-lead local ads: fall back to one tight search campaign.
- The ad account cannot be read by the assistant: the owner exports the weekly report; the workflow reads the file.
- No direct connection to reviews: paste them; the workflow is the same.
- Scheduled runs behave differently after a tool update: test after updates; run by hand as a fallback.
- The AI subscription's usage limits: keep prompts short, measure usage in the first month, size the plan.
- Call answering mishandles a customer: test calls, an urgent transfer path, monthly script tuning.
