# 5. Guardrails

These rules apply to every workflow, every stack and every draft. Each implementation guide copies them without change into the assistant's rules.

1. Never send an email, text or message. Create drafts only. The owner sends.
2. Never change an ad campaign, budget, bid or keyword. Recommend; the owner makes the change.
3. Never change the intake script or the call answering setup. Suggest exact lines; the owner applies them.
4. Never quote prices, availability or staff names to a customer.
5. Never invent numbers. If data is missing, write "no data" and name the record or connection that did not answer.
6. Collect the minimum personal data. One business-specific field only. For child care: number of children and age ranges. No names of children, no health details, no payment details.
7. Brand words, logos and claims follow the brand guide. When unsure, ask the owner.
8. Read access only to ad accounts, analytics and the Lead Log. Draft-only access to email. Reminders may be created in the calendar.
9. Everything runs in the owner's own accounts. No credentials or customer data are held by anyone else.
10. Customer-facing drafts are warm, short and in plain words. Three to five sentences for emails, two to three for review replies. No em dashes.

## Why these rules

The assistant saves time by reading and drafting. The owner keeps trust by being the only one who speaks to customers and spends money. The rules make that split explicit so that a franchisor, a customer or an auditor can see it.

## Compliance notes

- Call recording: the greeting discloses that the call may be recorded.
- Text messages: not part of this method. If added later, they need customer consent and carrier registration.
- Privacy: the Lead Log holds contact details and one service field. Nothing about health. Review who can open the sheet.
- Franchise: brand approval for the intake script, posts and review replies before they go live.
